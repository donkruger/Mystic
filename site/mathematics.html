Battle resolution

The Die Is Cast

Every battle is two six-sided dice: overall strength = strength modifier + roll, highest total wins. That is 36 equally likely outcomes — few enough that we can simply count them. No simulation anywhere on this page: every probability is exact.

The 36 outcomes, at equal strength

Rows are the attacker’s roll, columns the defender’s. Win by 4 or more and the loser is destroyed; win by less and the defender retreats (the attacker must advance). Notice how much of the grid is not lethal.

    A triangular battlefield

    The margin of victory (attacker roll − defender roll) follows a triangular distribution: a dead heat is six times likelier than a rout. The coloured tails are the only cells where anyone dies at equal strength.

    Three uncomfortable facts

    • 8.3%of equal-strength battles destroy anything. The kill line (margin ≥ 4) sits near the edge of the dice.
    • 16.7%of equal-strength battles tie — an outcome the rulebook never defines.
    • 33.3%end in a retreat: the most common decisive result is a shuffle, not a kill.

    The ladder of advantage

    What a strength edge is worth, from −3 to +4. Each +1 shifts the whole triangular distribution one step sideways — which is why biome buffs are really dice surgery.

      Gold flow

      The Economy

      Gold enters through harvesting, biome tricks and selling creatures; it leaves through summoning and spells. Every player starts with 4 gold and a safety net of 1 gold for any turn ended creature-less.

      Harvest yields by biome

      Four tiles per biome. Eight biomes run 1–4 gold (mean 2.5); Desert alone runs 2–5 (mean 3.5) — the richest soil in the game, before its ±1 gold crossing effect is even counted.

      The price of power

      Ability costs from the master tables (mean 3.3 gold) — against a mean harvest of 2.61 gold per action. Selling a creature returns its strength in gold: mean 3.46.

      Exchange rates

      • 1 action≈ 2.61 gold when harvesting an average tile
      • 1 spell= 1 gold, no action — the cheapest tempo in the game
      • 1 creature= its strength in gold when sold (mean 3.46)
      • 1 Ocean entry= 0.67 gold in expectation for a native; a 1-in-6 double discard for a stranger

      The shuffle

      Deck & Draw Math

      Two decks: 90 creatures, and a Lands & Spells deck of 71 — 36 lands against only 35 spells. Every draw from it is a coin flip.

      The Lands & Spells deck

      50.7% of the deck is land. Your opening hand holds two of these cards, so the chance you can summon at all on turn one (gold permitting) is 76.1%. Nearly a quarter of players open without a deed.

      Reactions & triggers

      Lightning-bolt density: 12 of 27 abilities and 8 of 35 spells can be played in response. Most triggered abilities ask for a roll above 3 — a coin flip — or above 4 — one in three.

      The biome lottery

      Pair a random creature with a random land card and the biomes match only 11.1% of the time. Eight times out of nine, a summoning sends a creature into hostile terrain — the debuff chart is not an edge case, it is the default state of the board.

      The win condition

      The Chain

      Control 3–7 connected tiles, in any configuration, and hold them for a full round. “Any configuration” hides a combinatorial explosion — this page enumerates every free polyhex (winning shapes, up to rotation and reflection) live:

      Every winning shape, sizes 3 and 4

      Three trihexes and seven tetrahexes — drawn exactly as enumerated. By size 7 there are 333 distinct winning silhouettes.

      The growth of choice

      Free polyhexes by chain size. Longer chains are not just slower to build — the shape space explodes, which is why large-N games sprawl.

      Holding the line

      An attack at equal strength flips a tile 41.7% of the time (kill or retreat — the attacker must advance either way). Survival of a chain through a round of k such attacks is 0.583k — unless you defend on buffed ground, where Forest/Tundra’s +2 cuts the flip rate to 16.7%.

      The recommended chain length is 8 − players — a rule of thumb that dips below the 3-tile minimum at six players (8 − 6 = 2). Creators: consider max(3, 8 − n) in the next rulebook pass.

      For the creators

      Recommendations

      Six adjustments, each motivated by the numbers above. Daniel, Don — the dice have spoken.

      R1

      Tundra: Acclimatized +2 → +1

      At equal base strength, +2 turns a battle into 72.2% wins with a 27.8% kill rate — 3.3× the baseline kill rate — and a flat 0% chance of being destroyed. Worse, it stacks on the roster’s second-largest strength budget (43, mean 4.3): a Tundra native on home ice fights at an effective 6.3 against a world average of 3.46 — roughly a +3 differential, or 83.3% wins and 41.7% kills before spells enter the chat.

      At +1 the buff stays the best combat deal in the game (58.3% wins, 16.7% kills) without making home ice unwinnable for visitors. Alternative: keep +2 but pull Tundra’s strength budget down toward the 33–34 median.

      R2

      Define the tie

      16.7% of equal-strength battles — and 5.6–16.7% across the typical differential range — land on equal overall strength, an outcome the rulebook never rules on. That is roughly one battle in six paused for a table argument.

      Suggested ruling: a tie is a deadlock — the defender holds, the attacker does not advance. It costs nothing, favours the defender slightly, and matches the retreat-heavy spirit of the kill line.

      R3

      Mind the kill line (margin ≥ 4)

      On two d6, a 4-point kill margin means equal creatures destroy each other only 8.3% of the time; 33.3% of battles end in a retreat. The game is positional by design — creatures die to buffs and focus-fire, not to fair fights.

      If that grind is the intent, keep it — it is elegant. If you want bloodier battles, margin ≥ 3 doubles the parity kill rate to 16.7%. Do not do both this and keep Tundra at +2.

      R4

      Watch the Desert economy

      Desert holds the richest tiles (mean 3.5 gold, +40% over any other biome) and pays natives 1 gold every time a creature enters or leaves. A native crossing in and out nets +2 gold before harvesting — nearly a free harvest action (mean yield 2.61).

      Consider triggering Conditioned/Scorched on entry only, or once per creature per turn, so the desert pays for risk rather than for pacing back and forth.

      R5

      Balance Ocean in one currency

      Lucky pays gold (EV +0.67 per entry); Unlucky taxes cards (1-in-6 chance to discard two). Buff and debuff live in different economies, so the matchup is impossible to price and feels swingy rather than risky.

      Mirror the currencies: either both gold (e.g. Unlucky: on a 1, lose 2 gold) or both cards. The current EV asymmetry also makes Ocean the only biome whose debuff can be wholly avoided by simply having no cards — a perverse incentive to empty your hand.

      R6

      Close the data gaps

      Some questions no amount of enumeration can answer, because the data is not in the tables: creature summoning costs are specced on the card face but absent from Creatures_tbl (so cost-efficiency — the single most important balance metric — is uncomputable); Abilities_tbl.Cost contradicts the rulebook (“abilities cost no gold”); AbilityID 23 is missing; spell cost is flat-1 in the rules but per-spell in the renders.

      Add a summoning-cost column keyed to strength (we suggest cost ≈ strength − 1, mean 2.46 gold, as a starting point for playtesting) and resolve the three contradictions — then re-run this page.
